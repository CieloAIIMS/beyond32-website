H3: Am I a good candidate for teeth whitening?
P: You may be a good candidate for teeth whitening if you have healthy teeth and gums with stains from food, drinks or ageing. We’ll usually recommend a check-up and clean before whitening to remove plaque and tartar and to check for decay or gum issues.
P: Teeth whitening may not be suitable if you have untreated decay, gum disease, severe sensitivity or certain types of intrinsic staining. Old fillings, crowns and veneers also don’t change colour with whitening gel, so we may discuss other cosmetic options if needed.

H3: How do I look after my teeth after whitening?
H3: Choosing the right foods and drinks
P: In the first couple of days, your teeth are more prone to stains. Drinks such as coffee, tea, and red wine can discolour your enamel quickly during this period. Water and milk are safe options, and lighter-coloured foods like rice, chicken, or bananas are easier choices while your enamel settles. Avoid sauces and richly coloured meals for a short time, as this simple step supports longer-lasting results from your teeth whitening treatments.
H3: Brushing habits that support your results
P: Gentle brushing with a soft brush is essential after whitening. This protects your enamel from unnecessary wear, especially if you notice sensitivity. Whitening toothpaste can help reduce surface stains between appointments, but it should be alternated with fluoride-based pastes to maintain strength. Think of this as extending the benefits of your professional whitening while keeping your teeth healthy and comfortable.
H3: Why regular dental visits still matter
P: Whitening may feel like the end goal, but in truth, it is part of broader dental health. Routine dental visits allow us to check your gums, monitor tooth discolouration, and remove tartar that can interfere with whitening results. These appointments also give you the chance to discuss whether further care is needed. Linking your whitening results with your overall dental treatments ensures a brighter smile remains easier to maintain over time.
H3: Using take-home kits safely

This kit includes whitening gel and custom whitening trays designed to fit securely, reducing the chance of leakage and uneven coverage. Always follow the instructions provided, as using too much gel can irritate your gums rather than improve whitening results. By applying the whitening agent correctly, you will maintain a more even outcome that fits neatly into your routine.
H3: Everyday habits that help you maintain a brighter smile

Drinking darker beverages through a straw, rinsing with water after meals, and eating crunchy fruit like apples can all reduce new stains. Tobacco is one of the leading causes of tooth discolouration, and limiting its use can dramatically extend your results. These simple adjustments blend into your routine and give you more control over how long your brighter smile stays.

H3: How safe is professional teeth whitening?
P: It’s normal to feel cautious about whitening your teeth. Many people wonder if the process could harm their enamel or cause unnecessary discomfort. The truth is that professional whitening is designed with safety in mind, especially when it’s provided under the care of a dentist.
H3: The science behind whitening agents
P: The main ingredient used in whitening gels is a form of peroxide. This compound works by breaking down stains into smaller particles, which lightens the appearance of the tooth surface. Under professional supervision, the concentration is carefully chosen to balance effectiveness with safety. The gel is applied in a controlled way to reduce risks while still producing noticeable results.
H3: Why dentist supervision matters
P: Store-bought kits often use trays or strips that don’t fit properly, which can cause uneven coverage or gel leakage. In contrast, professional whitening involves custom trays or in-chair application by trained hands. This supervision means your gums are protected, the gel is used in the right amount, and treatment is stopped if any problems occur. Supervision is one of the key reasons professional whitening is considered far safer than trying it on your own.
H3: Managing sensitivity with care
P: Sensitivity is one of the most common side effects patients worry about. While some temporary discomfort may occur, our clinic provides protective measures such as desensitising gels and careful monitoring during treatment. Most patients find that any sensitivity is short-lived and manageable. This extra care helps you feel at ease during and after the appointment, knowing your comfort is always a priority.
H3: The risks of DIY whitening products
P: It can be tempting to try whitening toothpaste or over-the-counter kits first. While these products can sometimes help with surface stains, they don’t offer the same predictability as professional whitening. Some online kits may even contain ingredients that are too harsh, leading to gum irritation or damage to enamel. By choosing professional treatments, you avoid these unnecessary risks and gain the reassurance that comes with proper guidance.
H3: Linking whitening to overall dental health
P: Before any whitening treatment, a dental check up is recommended. This allows us to look for issues such as tooth decay, gum inflammation, or existing restorations that could affect results. For example, fillings or crowns do not respond to whitening gel in the same way natural teeth do. Addressing these concerns first ensures whitening is carried out safely and effectively, while also supporting your long-term dental health.
H3: Reassurance from professional dental care
P: Safety is the foundation of any treatment we provide. From the initial consultation to the final application, every step is designed to protect your teeth and gums while delivering a brighter appearance. With professional whitening, you’re not only improving the way your smile looks, but also starting a whitening journey that respects your comfort and long-term oral health.
H3: Can veneers be whitened?
P: Many people wonder if veneers can be whitened like natural teeth. The truth is that whitening gel has no effect on porcelain or composite materials. Unlike enamel, which responds to bleaching agents, veneers keep the same shade they were made in.
P: If your veneers look darker compared with nearby teeth, the colour change usually comes from the natural teeth around them, not the veneers themselves. This can create an uneven look. In such cases, whitening the surrounding teeth may restore a more even appearance, but the veneers themselves will remain the same.
P: When veneers no longer match your natural shade or have surface wear, the only option is to replace them. Regular dental visits and proper cleaning help you maintain a healthy smile and delay the need for replacement. Good oral care also prevents staining of natural teeth, reducing contrast with existing veneers.
P: If you’re considering veneers in the future, it’s often recommended to complete whitening first. This allows veneers to be created in a colour that blends well and supports a whiter smile overall. With exceptional care and planning, veneers can remain consistent and natural-looking for many years.
H3: What should I avoid eating or drinking after teeth whitening?
P: For the first 24–48 hours after whitening, your teeth are more prone to staining. We recommend avoiding:
LI: Dark drinks like coffee, tea, red wine, cola and coloured juices
LI: Strongly coloured foods such as curry, tomato-based sauces, beetroot and soy sauce
LI: Smoking or vaping, which can quickly re-stain teeth
P: Choose light-coloured foods such as chicken, rice, pasta and bananas, and drink mainly water or milk during this time to help protect your new shade.
