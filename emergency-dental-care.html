Severe toothache

Call us today

Throbbing, constant or keeping-you-up pain usually means the nerve is irritated or infected. It rarely settles on its own, so the sooner we look, the simpler it tends to be to fix.

Right now

  1. Rinse gently with warm salt water (half a teaspoon of salt in a glass of water).
  2. Floss around the tooth in case food is trapped and pressing on the gum.
  3. Take over-the-counter pain relief as directed on the packet.
  4. Hold a cold pack wrapped in a cloth against your cheek for 20 minutes at a time.

Try to avoid

  • Putting aspirin directly on the gum. It can burn.
  • Heat packs on your face
  • Very hot, cold or sugary food and drinks

Knocked-out tooth

Call us straight away

An adult tooth can often be saved if it’s back in place within the first hour, ideally within 30 minutes. Stay calm and move quickly.

Right now

  1. Find the tooth and pick it up by the white crown, never the root.
  2. If it’s dirty, rinse it for a few seconds in milk or saline. Don’t scrub it.
  3. If you can, gently push it back into the socket and bite on a clean cloth to hold it.
  4. If not, keep it in a small container of milk, or inside your cheek, and call us on the way.

Try to avoid

  • Storing the tooth in water or letting it dry out
  • Touching or wiping the root

Baby tooth? Don’t put it back in, as it can damage the adult tooth underneath. Keep it and call us.

Broken or cracked tooth

Call us today

Most chips and cracks can be repaired so they look and feel like nothing happened. If there’s sharp pain, or you can see pink or red in the middle of the tooth, let us know when you call.

Right now

  1. Rinse your mouth gently with warm water.
  2. Save any broken pieces in a little milk and bring them with you.
  3. Use a cold pack on your cheek if there’s swelling.
  4. Cover a sharp edge with sugar-free gum or dental wax from the pharmacy.

Try to avoid

  • Chewing on that side
  • Hard, crunchy or sticky foods

Abscess or swelling

Call us today

Swelling in the gum or face usually means infection. It needs a dentist to treat the cause, not just the symptoms, so please don’t wait for it to go down.

Right now

  1. Rinse with warm salt water a few times a day.
  2. Take over-the-counter pain relief as directed on the packet.
  3. Use a cold pack on the outside of your cheek, 20 minutes on, 20 off.
  4. Keep your head raised when you lie down.

Try to avoid

  • Squeezing or popping a gum boil
  • Heat packs, which can spread infection

Go to hospital if swelling makes it hard to breathe or swallow, or spreads to your eye or neck.

Accident or dental trauma

Call us straight away

Sport, falls, the playground. Teeth can be pushed out of position, loosened or broken. Check the person is otherwise OK first.

Right now

  1. Check for a head injury. If there’s confusion, vomiting or they blacked out, call 000.
  2. Rinse gently and apply a cold pack to reduce swelling.
  3. Collect any teeth or pieces of tooth and keep them in milk.
  4. Call us. Loose or pushed teeth are best repositioned early.

Try to avoid

  • Wiggling loose teeth to “check” them
  • Waiting to see if it settles

Possible broken jaw? If the bite suddenly feels wrong or the mouth won’t close, go to the emergency department.

Bleeding that won’t stop

Call us straight away

Some oozing after an extraction is normal. Steady bleeding that keeps filling your mouth isn’t. Firm, constant pressure stops most bleeding.

Right now

  1. Fold a clean piece of gauze, or a damp black-tea bag, into a firm pad.
  2. Place it over the area and bite down firmly for 20 minutes without checking.
  3. Sit upright and stay calm. Repeat once with fresh gauze if needed.
  4. If it’s still bleeding heavily after that, call us or go to the emergency department.

Try to avoid

  • Rinsing, spitting or using straws
  • Hot drinks, exercise and alcohol

On blood thinners? Mention it when you call. It helps us advise you properly.

Lost filling or crown

Call within a day or two

Not usually urgent unless it hurts, but the tooth underneath is more fragile without its cover, so it’s worth booking in soon.

Right now

  1. Keep the crown safe in a small container and bring it in. We can often re-cement it.
  2. Use temporary dental cement from the pharmacy to cover the gap if it’s sensitive.
  3. Chew on the other side until you see us.

Try to avoid

  • Superglue or household adhesives
  • Sticky lollies and chewing gum

When to go to hospital instead

These need urgent medical care. Call 000 or go to your nearest emergency department, then let us know so we can follow up.

  • Swelling that affects breathing or swallowing
  • Swelling spreading to the eye, jaw or neck
  • Heavy bleeding that won’t stop with pressure
  • A head injury, blackout, vomiting or confusion
  • A possible broken jaw
  • High fever with facial swelling

Emergency questions, answered

If you’re not sure whether it’s urgent, call anyway. We’d much rather hear from you.

Do you see emergency patients who aren’t registered with you?

Yes. You don’t need to be an existing patient. Call us, tell us what’s happened and we’ll do our best to see you the same day.

What if it happens after hours or on a Sunday?

Follow the “right now” steps above and call us as soon as we open. Leave a message and we’ll call you back first thing.

For anything in the “go to hospital” list, call 000 or visit your nearest emergency department. For general health advice at any hour, you can call healthdirect on 1800 022 222.

How much does an emergency appointment cost?

It depends on what you need. We’ll tell you the cost of the examination and any X-rays before we begin, and give you a written quote for any further treatment. We accept all major health funds and can claim on the spot.

Is a chipped tooth an emergency?

A small chip with no pain can usually wait a few days. If it’s sharp, sensitive, painful, or a large piece has broken off, call us today.

My child knocked out a baby tooth. What should I do?

Don’t try to put it back in. Rinse their mouth gently, use a cold compress for swelling and call us so we can check the adult tooth underneath hasn’t been affected.

Can I just get antibiotics?

Antibiotics can help with spreading infection, but they don’t fix the tooth causing it, so the problem usually comes back. We’ll treat the source so it doesn’t keep returning.
