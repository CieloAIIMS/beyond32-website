Daily brushing and flossing are the foundation of good oral health, but they cannot remove everything. Over time, plaque hardens into tartar, a calcified deposit that builds up along the gum line and between teeth, and can only be removed with professional dental instruments.

Once tartar forms, no amount of brushing at home will shift it.

A scale and clean is a professional dental cleaning procedure that removes plaque, tartar, and surface stains from all tooth surfaces, including the areas below the gum line that a toothbrush cannot reach. It is one of the most commonly recommended preventive treatments in dentistry and is often built into routine dental care.

Quick overview

  • A scale and clean is a professional dental procedure that removes plaque and tartar buildup from tooth surfaces and along the gum line.
  • It may benefit anyone who has not had a professional clean in the past six to twelve months, and is often recommended as part of regular dental check-ups.
  • The procedure may help reduce the risk of gum disease and tooth decay, though outcomes depend on existing oral health and home care habits.
  • How frequently a scale and clean is needed varies between individuals based on plaque accumulation, gum health, and lifestyle factors.
  • A dental assessment helps determine whether a standard clean is sufficient or whether additional treatment may be needed.

The sections below outline what a scale and clean involves, why it matters for your oral health, and what to expect during and after the appointment.

What is a scale and clean?

A scale and clean, also known as a professional dental cleaning, is a procedure in which a dentist removes accumulated plaque and tartar from the surfaces of the teeth using specialised instruments. It includes both scaling, the physical removal of deposits, and polishing, which smooths the tooth surface and removes surface stains.

Scaling can be performed using an ultrasonic scaler, which uses vibration to break up tartar deposits, or manual instruments, or a combination of both. The gum line and the spaces between teeth are given particular attention, as these are the areas where plaque tends to accumulate and where early gum disease most commonly begins.

Why is a scale and clean important?

Tartar buildup is the primary driver of gum disease. Once plaque hardens, it creates a rough surface where bacteria thrive and the gum tissue becomes irritated and inflamed. Left unmanaged, this can progress from gingivitis, the early and reversible stage of gum disease, to periodontitis, which causes damage to the bone and tissue supporting the teeth.

As the ADA oral hygiene policy statement confirms, plaque is the primary cause of both tooth decay and gum disease, and oral hygiene strategies that reduce plaque levels are central to preventing both conditions.

Regular professional cleaning reduces the bacterial load along the gum line and removes the conditions that allow gum disease to take hold. Professional dental check-ups and cleaning play an important role in catching and addressing oral health problems early, alongside daily brushing and flossing.

Beyond gum health, a scale and clean also removes surface staining from foods, drinks, and other lifestyle factors, and gives the dentist the opportunity to identify early signs of decay, cracked teeth, or other issues that may require attention.

What happens during a scale and clean appointment?

30–60 minA scale and clean appointment typically takes between 30 and 60 minutes, depending on the amount of tartar present and the overall condition of the teeth and gums.

The appointment generally involves:

  1. A visual examination of the teeth and gums before cleaning begins
  2. Scaling to remove plaque and tartar from all tooth surfaces, including areas below the gum line
  3. Polishing with a mildly abrasive paste to remove surface stains and smooth the tooth surface
  4. Rinsing to clear debris and residue
  5. A fluoride treatment in some cases, particularly for patients who are at higher risk of decay or who have tooth sensitivity
  6. Oral hygiene advice tailored to the individual patient

If the gums are inflamed or early gum disease is detected, the dentist may recommend more frequent appointments or additional treatment. In cases where tartar has accumulated in the deeper pockets between the teeth and gums, a more thorough procedure called root planing may be needed.

Most patients find the procedure straightforward. Some people with sensitive gums or significant tartar buildup may notice mild discomfort during scaling, particularly along the gum line, but this typically settles quickly after the appointment.

How often should you have a scale and clean?

6–12 monthsFor most adults, a scale and clean every six to twelve months is appropriate. However, this is a general guideline rather than a fixed rule.

Some people accumulate plaque and tartar more quickly than others. Those who smoke, have a history of gum disease, are managing systemic conditions such as diabetes, or have not had a professional clean for an extended period may benefit from more frequent appointments. Professional cleaning intervals should be based on individual gum disease risk, and a dentist will advise on the right schedule after assessing your specific circumstances.

Children and teenagers can also benefit from regular scale and cleans, as plaque buildup and early signs of gingivitis can develop at any age.

The best way to determine the right frequency for your situation is to have an assessment with your dentist, who can evaluate the current condition of your teeth and gums and advise accordingly.

What to expect after your appointment

Most people notice that their teeth feel smoother and their mouth feels cleaner immediately after a scale and clean. Some temporary sensitivity to hot and cold is common, particularly if tartar has been removed from around the gum line, but this usually resolves within a day or two.

Gums that were inflamed or bleeding before the appointment may take a week or so to settle after the cleaning. This is a normal part of the healing process, and regular cleaning over time generally reduces gum inflammation.

Gingivitis is the early stage of gum disease and can often be treated effectively with professional cleaning and home care. When more advanced periodontitis is present, professional cleaning remains an important part of long-term management, though additional treatment steps may also be required.

To maintain the results of a professional clean, consistent home care is essential.

That means brushing twice daily, cleaning between the teeth with floss or interdental brushes, and avoiding habits that promote plaque buildup.

When to book a scale and clean

If you have not had a professional dental clean in the past twelve months, or if you have noticed signs such as bleeding gums, persistent bad breath, or visible tartar deposits, it is worth booking an appointment.

Frequently asked questions

Does a scale and clean hurt?

Most patients find the procedure straightforward and experience little discomfort. Some sensitivity may occur around areas where tartar has accumulated near the gum line, particularly if the gums are already inflamed. A dentist will take care to work gently and can discuss options if you have concerns about sensitivity before the appointment begins.

What is the difference between a scale and clean and a regular clean at home?

Home cleaning with a toothbrush and floss removes soft plaque from the surfaces of the teeth. Once plaque hardens into tartar, it cannot be removed by brushing; only professional instruments can do this. A scale and clean removes both soft plaque and the hardened tartar deposits that accumulate over time, reaching areas between the teeth and along the gum line that home care cannot fully address.
