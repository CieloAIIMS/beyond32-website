Most broken teeth can be repaired so they look and feel like nothing happened.

A broken tooth is more common than people think, and it is rarely as bad as it feels in the moment. Depending on the break, the fix might be a single appointment to rebuild a chipped edge, or a crown to protect a tooth that has cracked.

What matters most is getting it looked at early. A small crack treated this week is a simpler, cheaper repair than the same tooth after it splits.

What to do right now

Until you can see a dentist, here are a few things that help. Then call us.

  1. Rinse your mouth

    Thoroughly but gently, with warm water, to clean the area and let you see the damage.

  2. Stop any bleeding

    Apply firm pressure to the area with clean gauze or a damp tea bag for ten to twenty minutes.

  3. Use a cold pack

    Hold it on the cheek or lips over the broken tooth to reduce swelling and relieve pain.

  4. Take pain relief

    Over-the-counter pain relief, as directed on the packet. Do not put tablets directly on the gum.

Save any pieces

Keep any broken fragments in a small container of milk and bring them with you. Occasionally a clean piece can be bonded straight back on.

If the whole tooth has come out, pick it up by the crown, never the root.

Try to avoid

  • Chewing on that side of your mouth
  • Very hot, very cold or sugary food and drinks
  • Running your tongue over a sharp edge. Cover it with sugar-free gum or dental wax from the pharmacy instead
  • Superglue or any household adhesive to reattach a piece
  • Waiting to see if it settles

Chipped, cracked or broken?

How deep the damage goes decides how we repair it. A tooth has three layers: hard outer enamel, softer dentine underneath, and the pulp at the centre, where the nerve and blood supply sit.

  1. 1
    Chipped tooth

    A small piece of enamel has broken off, usually from a front tooth edge. Often painless, sometimes sharp to the tongue. Usually repaired with bonding in one visit.

  2. 2
    Cracked tooth

    A crack runs into the dentine. It may hurt when you bite or release, or with cold. A crack will not heal on its own and tends to spread, so it usually needs a filling or crown to hold the tooth together.

  3. 3
    Broken to the nerve

    The break reaches the pulp. You may see pink or red, and it is often very sensitive. This needs to be seen the same day, and may need root canal treatment before the tooth is rebuilt.

  4. +
    Broken cusp or split tooth

    A back tooth with a large old filling can lose a corner, or split down the middle. A broken cusp is usually crowned. A tooth split below the gum line sometimes cannot be saved, and we will talk you through replacing it.

How we fix a broken tooth

Depending on the break, there is a range of treatment options.

We always start with the most conservative repair that will last, and we will show you why if a bigger one is needed.

Tooth-coloured bonding

For chips and small breaks

For smaller breaks, we can usually repair the tooth with composite resin matched to the colour of your teeth. It is sculpted on, set with a light and polished, often in under an hour and without an injection. Most people will never know you had the break.

Veneers

For front teeth with larger chips

A veneer sits on the front of the tooth and fills in the space left by the break. It is matched to the surrounding teeth, and some patients choose to treat several teeth at once as part of a smile makeover.

About veneers →

Crowns

For cracked, heavily filled or broken back teeth

A crown covers the whole remaining tooth, holding a cracked tooth together and protecting it from splitting. Modern ceramic crowns are made to be as strong and reliable as a natural tooth, and to match the teeth beside them.

About crowns →

Root canal treatment

For breaks that reach the nerve

If the pulp is exposed or infected, we clean and seal the inside of the tooth first, then rebuild it, usually with a crown. It lets us keep your own tooth rather than removing it.

About root canal treatment →

Implants

For teeth that cannot be saved

When a tooth is too badly broken to keep, an implant replaces it. Unlike dentures, which are removable, implants are fixed into the jaw bone, look and feel like your own tooth, and last for a very long time.

Will fixing it hurt?

Usually far less than the break itself.

Small chips are often repaired without any anaesthetic at all. For anything deeper, we numb the area first, so you feel pressure rather than pain.

If you are anxious, or it is a child who has had a fright, tell us. We go at your pace, explain each step before we do it, and have happy gas and in-house sedation available.

My child has broken a tooth. What should I do?

Check they are otherwise OK first. If there is a head injury, vomiting or confusion, call 000.

Rinse their mouth gently, use a cold compress for swelling, keep any pieces in milk and call us. If a baby tooth has been knocked out, do not try to put it back in, as it can damage the adult tooth developing underneath. We will check that the adult tooth has not been affected.

Can a cracked tooth heal on its own?

No. Enamel does not grow back, and a crack tends to spread a little more with every bite. The pain can come and go, which is why cracked teeth are often left too long. Treating it early with a filling or crown often saves a tooth that would otherwise split.

What does it cost to fix a broken tooth?

It depends on the repair. Bonding costs the least, while crowns and implants cost more.

Avoiding the next one

Most broken teeth come from a handful of very preventable habits.

Wear a mouthguard for sport

A custom-fitted guard spreads the force of a knock far better than a boil-and-bite one. Any contact sport counts, at any age.

Wear a night guard if you grind

Grinding and clenching put huge force through teeth every night, and are a common cause of cracks in back teeth.

Do not use your teeth as tools

Opening packets, cracking nuts, biting nails or chewing pens and ice all put edge-on force on teeth.

Watch large old fillings

A tooth that is mostly filling is a tooth waiting to crack. We may suggest a crown before it breaks rather than after.

Keep up your check ups

We often spot small cracks and weak fillings before they fail. See our check up and clean.

Get pain checked early

Pain on biting that comes and goes is a classic sign of a crack. It is much easier to fix before the tooth splits.

Is a broken tooth an emergency?

It is urgent if the tooth is painful, bleeding, loose, knocked out, or you can see pink or red in the middle of it. Call us the same day. A small chip with no pain can usually wait a few days for a normal appointment.

Can a chipped tooth be fixed in one visit?

Usually, yes. Most small to moderate chips on front teeth can be repaired with tooth-coloured composite bonding in under an hour.

Can a cracked tooth heal on its own?

No. Enamel does not grow back, and a crack tends to spread with chewing. Treating it early with a filling or crown often saves a tooth that would otherwise split.
